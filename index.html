How the body learns what hurts, what itches, and how to heal.

The Dong Lab studies the sensory neurons that detect pain, itch, and tissue damage — and is turning that biology into new medicines, from itch receptor drugs to stem cell–derived nerve cell therapies.

Signal propagation, always in motion — like the neurons we study

Xinzhong Dong, Ph.D. — Professor of Neuroscience · Investigator, Howard Hughes Medical Institute

Mouse sensory neurons in dorsal root ganglion (DRG)

Research Focus

Decoding the molecular basis of itch, pain, and neuroimmune signaling.

The lab takes a multidisciplinary approach to the primary sensory neurons that initiate pain and itch, and to the mast cells and neutrophils that interact with them. Work spans receptor discovery, in vivo imaging, and translation into new therapeutics — including as a scientific founder of two biotechnology companies.

01 · Receptors

Identification of itch receptors

Discovered the Mrgpr family of GPCRs as itch receptors in sensory neurons — including MrgprA3 for chloroquine-induced itch and MrgprX4 for bile-acid and bilirubin–driven cholestatic itch — opening new avenues for anti-itch drugs.

02 · Circuits

Identification of itch-sensing neurons

Showed for the first time that dedicated itch-sensing neurons (MrgprA3+) exist separately from pain neurons, resolving a century-old debate, and mapped how the spinal cord codes itch versus pain intensity.

03 · Immunity

Mast cell & neutrophil receptors

Identified MrgprB2/X2 as the mast-cell receptor behind pseudo-allergic drug reactions, neurogenic inflammation, and post-stroke brain inflammation, and MrgprA1/A2 as neutrophil receptors that guard against infection.

04 · Ion channels

Pirt & Pirt2: TRP channel regulators

Discovered Pirt and Pirt2 (Tmem100) as regulators of the TRPV1, TRPA1, and TRPM8 channels that govern heat, cold, and chronic pain sensitivity — revealing new targets for analgesics.

05 · In vivo imaging

DRG imaging & persistent pain

Created Pirt-GCaMP reporter mice for in vivo imaging of sensory neuron activity, uncovering mechanisms of chronic pain such as coupled and sympathetically-driven cluster firing of sensory neurons.

06 · Cell therapy

Stem cell–derived nociceptors for osteoarthritis

Engineered CD200high human stem cell–derived nociceptors (hPSC-NNs) that, transplanted into osteoarthritic joints, sequester inflammatory ligands, promote bone and cartilage repair, and relieve pain — a disease-modifying cell therapy for chronic pain where conventional mono-targeted analgesics fail.

Xinzhong Dong, Ph.D.

Principal Investigator · HHMI Investigator

Professor in the Solomon H. Snyder Department of Neuroscience, with joint appointments in Neurosurgery, at Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine. Dr. Dong's lab studies the molecular basis of itch and pain and translates those discoveries into new therapeutics, including as a scientific co-founder of Escient Pharmaceuticals and SereNeuro Therapeutics.
